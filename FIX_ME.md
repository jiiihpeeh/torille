### 1. `4a9e126` -- seven class modules overrode the flag

`inflect_in` documents the contract at `src/expand/mod.rs`: `possessives`
says whether the word's class takes possessive suffixes -- a noun does, an
adjective or participle does not. Seven classes took that argument and passed a
literal `true` to the builder or to a delegating call:

| class | what it did |
|---|---|
| 36 | `Builder::new(lemma, true)` in `superl_inflect` |
| 37 | -- nothing of its own; reaches the class-36 engine |
| 38 | `inflect_with_stems(lemma, true, ...)` |
| 39 | `Builder::new(lemma, true)` in `use_pallas` only -- `use_ude` and `inflect_graded_nom_base` already forwarded it correctly |
| 41 | `Builder::new(lemma, true)` |
| 45 | `Builder::new(lemma, true)`, plus `class_39::inflect(lemma, true)` for `kymmenes`/`tuhannes` |
| 49 | `Builder::new(lemma, true)`, plus `a_stem_inflect(lemma, true, ...)` |

Class 38 was the bulk of it by volume: 4491 lemmas, 3810 (84%) carrying
`adjektiivi`. The old comments justified the literal ("the superlatives are
also substantives, so keep the whole possessive column"), but a local guess is
strictly worse than the caller's: only the dictionary knows that `marto` is an
adjective that can still be a noun (`adjektiivi, substantiivi`) while `mieto`
is not.

Class 39 is the clearest evidence it was never deliberate -- `use_ude` and
`use_pallas` sit a few lines apart and disagree.

### 3. `2bc71d3` -- the Python binding's flag was a constant `true`

### 4. `74da243` -- `Builder::comitative` had no `possessives` guard

### 6. `9ad8772` -- a possessive form was tagged as not being a noun

The binding-vs-binary gate reported `pieni`, and following it up found the worse
thing behind it. `inflect_in` re-tags every form of a grid with the requested
part of speech, and for `pieni` the reading came from the *first* entry of its
sanalista field, `adjektiivi, substantiivi`. So the adjective reading was
stamped over the whole class-26 grid -- including `pieneemme`, a form carrying
the `-mme` suffix and tagged `adjective`. The right word, the right case, and a
reading that contradicts the suffix. Same shape as `ehtooheni` being tagged a
bare singular illative.

It was also self-contradictory inside one function. The possessive column was
already decided correctly, by looking for `substantiivi` anywhere in the raw
field; the reading was decided independently, by taking the field's first
entry. For `pieni` one said "takes possessives" and the other said
"adjective". Two decisions that have to agree, each defensible alone.

One rule now decides both, in `PartOfSpeech::is_substantival` /
`inflecting_reading` next to `parse_all`, which is where the rule belongs, with
the binding and the `inflect` binary both calling it. Anything not noun-capable
keeps `parse`'s first reading, so `mieto` (`adjektiivi`) is still an adjective
and still takes no possessives.

`pos` only ever re-tags, so no form changed: **0 lost, 0 gained**, and 538
lemmas changed digest with identical counts, which is the signature of a pure
reading correction. 521 have one field admitting a noun but leading with another
reading; 17 are homonyms whose noun reading is in a second row.

### 7. `cc0afee` -- a class-50 compound with no split inflected as one word

The class-50 engine inflects a compound part by part: the tail carries the case,
the prefix is fixed. `data/kotus-tyypit.txt` states what the number means --
`isoäiti : isoäidin` is 50 because the *initial* part stays uninflected, where
`hienosokeri : hienonsokerin` is 51 because it does not.

`find_parts` finds the two parts by looking for a tail that is a lemma of its
own. That fails when the head is not one the sanalista lists, and when it fails
the code fell through to applying the class-50 *model* (`isoisä`) to the whole
compound. The model then aligns the compound against itself and lands the
gradation in the middle of it: `ahkeraliisa` came back with 168 forms including
`ahkeraldin` and `ahkeraleilla`; `vapaaherratar` with `vapaaheeilla`. The heads
are `liisa`, `herratar`, `jaakko` and `heikki`, and **none of the four is in the
sanalista at all**.

The fix falls back to the head's declared class, which the sanalista names
beside the marker (`ahkeraliisa` is `9, 50`). Measured over the 22 fingerprint
lemmas carrying a 50/51 marker and having reference forms -- `ok='both'`,
non-possessive only, see the traps -- the four failures go:

| word | list | before | after | nonsense forms gone |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `vapaaherratar` | `32*C,50` | 1% | 100% | 167 |
| `laiskajaakko` | `1*A,50` | 32% | 100% | 156 |
| `helppoheikki` | `5*A,50` | 45% | 100% | 135 |
| `ahkeraliisa` | `9,50` | 52% | 100% | 155 |

Class 51 is excluded deliberately: it asserts the opposite, so the whole word is
not its head's paradigm and falling back would be wrong rather than useless.
Nothing needs it anyway -- 91 of the 93 class-51 words in the sanalista split,
and both that do not declare no head class either.

### 8. `4a13ce3` -- the `inflect` binary took only the first class

The gate failed on 3 of its 21 probe words, and this is the only gate in the
project that can catch a defect *both* consumers share -- the tree-equivalence
gate compares two trees and passes when they are wrong the same way.

`pieni` and `ahkeraliisa` were fixed in `9ad8772` and `cc0afee`. `ori` was this
commit: the binary took `l.inflections().next()`, the first class of the first

different code path in each consumer even once the class count matched.

**The gate is now green: all 21 lemmas agree.**

### 9. `97de6d1` -- a form was said twice

The sanalista can list one word under two classes whose grids coincide, and both
callers concatenated the two grids without looking at the result. `ahtaus` is
two homonym rows, `40` and `39`, and its 318 forms are 159 words each appearing

### 10. `0f12bbb` -- a field that is only a bracketed class had no class at all

### 11. `ba4b738` -- a compound tail had to be three letters long

`find_parts` applied a three-letter minimum to *both* halves of a class-50/51
compound. Nothing in the sanalista is one letter long, so the floor read as

### 12. A merge put one core's base classes on another core's base words

`FormBuilder::merge` reads the other half's base tables at the *other half's* base
number:

```rust
for (id, classes) in other.base_classes.iter().enumerate() {
    if self.base_classes[id].is_empty() {          // <- `id` is `other`'s number
```

`other` numbered its own bases in its own order, so `other`'s `id` is reached
here as `base_of[id]` -- the whole point of the loop immediately above it. The
tables stay exactly as long as `base_words`, so nothing panics and nothing looks
wrong; every base in a merged builder simply carries whichever other base's
classes happened to land on its number. `build` splits the lexicon into shares
and merges them, so on any machine with more than one core this is the normal
path, not an edge case.

**It went unnoticed because `base_classes` and `base_poses` had no readers.**
`grep` over `src/`, `bindings/` and `tools/` finds no consumer outside
`build.rs` itself; the reverse-lookup API added this session is the first code to
ask. That is the reason it is worth fixing now rather than when it bites: the
ranking added below reads exactly this table, and ranking every base by
somebody else's class is not a ranking.

The fix is `base_of[id]`, and the test
`a_merge_keeps_each_base_with_its_own_classes` builds two halves **by hand** and
merges them, so it does not depend on how many cores the machine has -- a test of
a merge should not need the merge to be reached through `build`'s threshold. The
second half is the *smaller* one on purpose: that is the shape in which the two id
spaces disagree, since a base numbered 0 arrives as number 2. Reverting the fix
makes it fail with `left: [("lapsi", [])]`, `right: [("lapsi", [29])]`.

The class filter right above it was duplicated inline as
`class == 99 || class == 100 || class == 101`; it now calls
[`kotus::carries_a_model`], the predicate item 13's prior needs anyway.

**No fingerprint change and no re-baseline** -- `v23 == v20` byte for byte, and
that is a real "nothing changed" rather than one of the two blind spots below,
because nothing that emits a form reads these tables.

### The gate for the other direction

The three existing gates all look the same way: the fingerprint digests the
**forward** direction, the equivalence gate compares the two trees, and the
possessive gate checks one column of the output. None of them ever asks whether
the index can answer *"which word is this form of"* correctly -- and the
fingerprint structurally cannot, since a form that goes in and comes back
attached to the wrong base produces exactly as many forms as before.

`tools/test_reverse_lookup_recall.py` is that gate, with the thresholds in
Python and the arithmetic in Rust (`tools/src/bin/lookup_recall.rs`), so a
measurement cannot grade itself. It asks two separate questions, because they are
two:

    reference (lemma, form) pairs: 626201
      the right base is among the answers    624914   99.79%
      first base right, sanalista order       605796   96.74%
      first base right, ranked                609971   97.41%
      words with more than one base form      35990    5.75%

This block is `a93f744`'s measurement, printed as it stood then. Item 14 later
moved the first, second and third lines -- to 625 236 / 99.85%, 606 117 / 96.79%
and 610 292 / 97.46% -- and raised the floor to 99.8%. Item 15 moved them again, to
what the gate prints now:

```
reference (lemma, form) pairs: 626201
  the right base is among the answers    625367   99.87%
  first base right, sanalista order       606236   96.81%
  first base right, ranked                610407   97.48%
  words with more than one base form      36034    5.75%
```

The last line moved too, and not because of either fix: both changed which forms
a lemma makes or which base a form points at, not how many bases it can have. The
42 extra pairs are pronouns whose forms other pronouns also produce.

Recall is the direction the engine gets credit for, and only recall: the reference
does not list a form as wrong, so absence is unconfirmed rather than incorrect
and precision is not measurable this way at all. It compares like with like
(`ok='both'`, `possessive=''`, nominative dropped) for the reasons
`compound_recall.py` gives.

The second and third lines are why 96.74% is not a defect: **5.75% of the
reference's `(lemma, form)` pairs carry more than one base form**, so a gate that
scored only the first base would be measuring ambiguity. (This file said 5.4% at
`a93f744`; the gate prints 5.75% and 35 990 of 626 201 pairs, which is 5.75%, so
5.4% was a transcription slip and never a separate measurement. Corrected.)
Ranking is a guess, and a guess gets measured against the alternative it replaces
rather than argued for -- so the
gate *fails if the ranking is worse* and does not fail if it is merely no better,
with the difference printed for a human to judge. It is worth **0.67 points**
here.

The floor was **99.7%**, against a measured 99.79%: the headroom was the two open
defects below, worth 0.21 points between them (1 287 of 626 201 pairs), so this
gate would not catch them again on its own and does not pretend to. Item 14 took
322 of them, which is 0.05 points; item 15 took a further 131, which is 0.02. The
floor is now **99.8%** against a measured **99.87%**, leaving **834** pairs of
known-open defect, or 0.13 points. What the gate catches is a regression -- a
linking change that loses a base form, or a ranking that starts filtering rather
than reordering, which is the one failure a rank function can have that is silent.

### The ranking prior, and why it ignores 99

`ClassPrior` (`src/expand/build.rs`) is a count of how many lemmas the sanalista
gives each class, and a base is scored by the size of its *commonest* class.
Ranks are stable, so ties keep the sanalista's own order rather than an
arbitrary one.

It lives in the **core**, not in the CLI or the binding, as
`ranked_bases_of`/`ranked_bases_in` over `bases_with_classes`/
`bases_in_with_classes`. This project has already fixed one binding/binary
duplication, so a second one was not going to be added for a ranking: the
`lookup` binary and the Python binding must give the same order, and there is
now nowhere for them to diverge. `bases_of`/`bases_in` are thin wrappers, so
each traversal has exactly one implementation.

`99`, `100` and `101` are excluded from both the count and the score, by
[`kotus::carries_a_model`], which cites `data/kotus-tyypit.txt:55` -- they mark a
word rather than declining it. This was not a refinement; it was a bug in the
first version. `99` is what **5 832** sanalista lemmas carry, so counting it as a
declension made markers the *best* explanation of any word they appeared in:
`lookup vastaan` returned `vastaan [99]`, `vasta [9]`, `vastata [73]` -- the
adverb in front of the noun and the verb. With the rule it is `vasta [9]`,
`vastata [73]`, `vastaan [99]`.

Note that `carries_a_model` is deliberately **not** `Lemma::is_indeclinable`.
They are different questions -- "does this number stand for a model" versus "is
*this word* marked uninflecting" -- and `100` does not answer the second one the
same way. Keeping them apart is what leaves `carries_a_case`, and so every
compound tail test, untouched.

**It is now also not the whole of what it is used for**, which is the point of
item 15. `carries_a_model` answers "is this a declension?", and the *prior* wants
exactly that answer: a marker must not outrank a real class. The *index* asks a
different question -- "are there forms of this word worth reaching?" -- and for a
pronoun the answer is yes while the first answer is still no. So the two roles
have been split into [`kotus::carries_a_model`] (unchanged, still `99`/`100`/`101`
for the prior) and [`kotus::has_own_paradigm`] (`101` only, for the index).
Splitting them rather than widening the first is what leaves
`a_class_that_marks_a_word_is_not_a_class_to_score_on` still asserting that all
three markers score zero.

### 14. `11a1169` -- a `-inen` loanword's harmony was read off the wrong syllable

`front()` scans the whole lemma right to left for the last `a/o/u/ä/ö/y`, with `e`
and `i` neutral. Class 38's `-inen`/`-llinen`/`-ttinen` suffixes are **nothing but
neutral vowels**, so for any word ending in one the scan walks straight through
the suffix and into whatever precedes it. For a Finnish compound that is usually
the right answer -- the head sits in front of the suffix. For a loanword it is
not: `biogeeninen` contains no Finnish back vowel anywhere except the `o` of
`geo`, and that is what ordinary harmony finds, so the reference's `biogeeninen`
-> `biogeenises`**`ssä`** came out `biogeenisessa`.

**23 lemmas.** Measured by asking for the inessive singular of all 3 342 class-38
lemmas the reference double-confirms: **3 319** already right, **23** wrong, and
**0** wrong the other way. The engine is never too front here, only too back --
which is what makes it a heuristic miss rather than a compound-head question.
All 23 are in the fingerprint's lemma set, so this one **did** move the digest.

The shape of the fix is not new, and that is the point: `harmony.rs` already
carries `FRONT_EXCEPTIONS` for the `-i` loanword family, documented with exactly
this reason -- `-geeni`, `-metri` and `-ismi` "mix in the reference
(`antigeeni` vs `allergeeni`, `dynamometri` vs `kronometri`)". So `FRONT_CLASS_38`
sits beside it and `class_38_harmony` is ordinary harmony with the measured
exceptions layered over the top. There is no head stage, because no productive
head governs these: `ajanvietteellinen` and `laajaspektrinen` have
Finnish-looking first parts (`aja`, `laaja`) that are both back, and the
reference still makes the whole word front.

**This is a word list, and it is worth being honest about why one is needed.** The
suffix predicts nothing: grouped by the three letters before `nen`, the
reference's own harmony mixes within every ending -- `-llinen` is 442 back and 1
front, `-ttinen` 110 and 1, `-meinen` 4 and 5, `-trinen` 6 and 5, `-eni` 8 and 8.
So no rule over the ending separates them. The sanalista has no harmony column
for a word to be wrong in, and **22 of the 23 have no sanalista base to inherit
one from** (`biogeeni` is not a lemma; `mutageeni`, which is, is *already* in
`FRONT_EXCEPTIONS`). A word added to the list has to have been measured, and
`the_class_38_exception_list_is_the_measured_one` pins the count at 23 so the list
cannot quietly grow. `every_listed_class_38_word_is_front` is deliberately paired
with `an_unlisted_class_38_word_keeps_its_own_harmony`, because a list that only
ever added front harmony would pass the first test while quietly turning the whole
class front -- which is the shape of failure worth guarding against here.

```
ajanvietteellinen  antisemiittinen  antiseptinen       biogeeninen
biometrinen         egosentrinen      erogeeninen       heterogeeninen
homogeeninen        isometrinen       laajaspektrinen   moni-ilmeinen
monoteistinen       mutageeninen      muuntogeeninen   omailmeinen
polikliininen       psykogeeninen     psykometrinen    rikasilmeinen
siirtogeeninen      tiukkailmeinen    vakavailmeinen
```

The grid itself did not change. `inflect_with_stems` now delegates to a private
`consonant_word`, and its sibling `inflect_with_stems_harmony` hands it a
`Builder::new_harmony` instead -- so the two entry points differ in nothing but
the builder that picks the endings, which is what keeps a harmony exception from
being able to touch a stem. Class 32's own callers are untouched.

**Re-baselined to `nouns_base_v24`** (`292380db5c48338c1d5098dcbd5e5019`).
**23 lemmas changed, forms lost 0, forms gained 0** -- the total is identical

with it, 99.7% -> **99.8%**: a fix should tighten its own floor, and that is

### 16. A class-10 lemma ending in `-n` is not a stem, and `class_10` is now ported

The gate's misses for `kahdeksan`, `seitsemän` and `yhdeksän` were not a gradation
problem or a missing ending. They were the whole paradigm, built on the wrong
base:

```
$ ./target/release/inflect kahdeksan
kahdeksanen  kahdeksanin  kahdeksanilla  kahdeksannn  kahdeksantta ...
```

`class_10` opens with `let stem = lemma;`, which is right for `tuhka` and wrong
for these three. `i_stem` maps a stem's final `a`/`ä` to `i`, so a class-10 stem
*ends in `a`/`ä` by definition* -- a lemma ending in `n` cannot be one. On
`kahdeksan` the `i_stem` was a no-op, so the plural endings were appended straight
onto the lemma and the `-n` was counted twice.

**The population is three lemmas, and it was measured before the rule was
written.** Of the 2 659 lemmas `parse_inflections` puts in class 10, exactly
**three end in `n`**, all three numerals, and all three were 22 of 23 reference
forms short. So the rule is a shape rule with a stated population:

```rust
let bare = lemma.strip_suffix('n').filter(|s| s.ends_with(['a', 'ä']));
let stem = bare.unwrap_or(lemma);
```

The `.filter` is not decoration: "a class-10 stem ends in `a`/`ä`" is the premise
the rule rests on, and requiring it means the rule cannot fire on a `-n` lemma
that is not this shape, whatever the sanalista grows into.

**Two decisions inside the fix, both measured.**

*Which stem gets the nominative.* The lemma is the citation form and
`kahdeksan` is the genitive cell (`{stem}n`), so nothing is lost by not offering
a nominative -- but what *would* be offered is `kahdeksa`, and that is not a
word: `kahdeksa`, `seitsemä` and `yhdeksä` appear in the reference **neither as
lemmas nor as forms of anything**, 0 rows each. Offering them would have added
three non-words to fix a defect about non-words. So the nominative cell is
dropped where the `-n` was stripped, and the test
`the_stem_under_a_stripped_ending_is_never_offered_as_a_word` is what holds it.

*Whether the `ok='both'` possessives are missing.* The reference lists 12
possessive forms per numeral (`kahdeksastaan`, `kahdeksastani`, ...) and the
engine emits none. They are `kahdeksasta` + `-ni`, mechanically: a numeral has
no possessive suffix in Finnish, and the reference's own column calls `kahdeksani`
a "nominative singular, first person possessive", which is not a form of
anything. Left alone, and out of the gate's scope anyway -- it reads
`possessive=''`.

**66 pairs, 99.87% -> 99.88%** (625 367 -> 625 433), top-1 up 96.81% -> 96.82%
plain and 97.48% -> 97.49% ranked. **Re-baselined to `nouns_base_v25`**:
3 lemmas, 16 -> 26 forms each, **41 lost and 71 gained**, net +30 (4 162 054 ->
4 162 084). Every one of the 41 lost is an artefact of the old stem and is not a
word -- `kahdeksanen`, `kahdeksannn`, `kahdeksanineen`, `kahdeksantta` -- and the
71 gained are the reference's 22 plus the lemma and the class's own `rare` short
genitive. The arithmetic is the check: -41 + 71 = +30, which is exactly the
change in the fingerprint's total.

#### And the class is swapped, so this fix is in one file

`class_10` is now `#[path]`-pointed at the refactored tree and
`src/expand/nominals/class_10.rs` is **deleted**, the way `class_1` and `utility`
already were. The port was the cheaper half of this change: the two
implementations agree on the shape of this defect already, because it is the same
grammar in two idioms, so the fix was written once and the *swap* is what put it
in both trees -- which is a better answer to "both trees in the same commit" than
a careful edit twice.

Three things are worth writing down about a swap.

- **The port was clean here, and that is a property of the class, not of the
  port.** Both files exposed the same four public items with the same signatures
  (`CLASS`, `inflect`, `inflect_graded`, `inflect_graded_with`) and all twelve

  Item 6's warning -- that `utility` had to land first because class 1 could not

### 17. `1f09afd` -- compound splitting read the whole lexicon, and now reads a table

Not a gate finding. This one was asked for: *"we need a lookup_base function that
uses efficient scanning"*, and then *"make the index compiletime"*. Both requests
were right about the code and wrong about where the time went, and the
measurements are below.

**The scan that was there.** A class 50/51 compound is split by looking for the
words it is made of, and that was a `for head in lexicon` over all 104 743
lemmas per compound with a second full scan nested inside it. 696 compounds, so
the head test ran about **73 million times**. There is no `lookup_base` in the
codebase and there never was -- `starts_with` appears only in morphological
logic (`kotus.rs`, `compound.rs`, `gradation.rs`, `spec.rs`, `inflect.rs`,
`expand/mod.rs`), never as a word-lookup facility. The four scan sites were
`find_parts` (a class-51 head loop and a class-50 tail loop), `compound_heads`,
and `head_class`.

**The observation that makes it a range query.** A head is a *prefix* of the
compound and a tail is a *suffix*, so neither question is "search the lexicon" --
both are "find the run of words under this prefix". `build.rs` now sorts the rows
once, at compile time, into `WORD_ORDER` (by the word) and `TAIL_ORDER` (by the
word **reversed** -- reversing is what turns a suffix into a prefix, so the
suffix question is the same search and there is no reversed copy of the text in
the binary). Each is a `partition_point` plus a scan while the bound holds.
Nothing is built at run time: `LexiconIndex` is one borrow wide, asserted by
`the_lexicon_needs_no_index_built_at_run_time`.

**Measured, 696 compounds, each split twice, single-threaded:**

| | time | answers |
|---|---|---|
| linear scan | 4.30 s | |
| sorted-order search | 3.24 ms | identical, **0 disagreements** |

**The index build as a whole did not get faster: ~38 s either way, and it is
worth not overselling this.** That is arithmetic rather than a disappointment.
The 4.30 s is *one thread*, and `build()` runs on twelve, so the scan was about
**0.36 s of wall time out of 38** -- under 1%. It was never the bottleneck. What
the change buys is the **shape**, not the seconds: a lookup is O(log n) instead of
O(lemmas), so adding words to the sanalista stops costing time quadratically.
The binary grows about 2 MB for the two orders.

**Why a precomputed table and not a `HashMap`.** The obvious fix -- bucket the
lexicon by prefix, keep the buckets -- was written and measured first, and it was
**worse**: 37 ms to build per process, and it made the whole build *slower*
(38-42 s against 32-33 s). `build.rs` already emits a deterministic FNV-1a table
for exact word lookup, so the runtime `words` map was duplicating something that
was already there; that is now the untouched `Sanalista::by_word`. Two lessons
worth keeping: a per-process structure has to be paid for on every run of a tool
that inflects one word, and "it is obviously faster" is not a measurement.

**Three bugs the tests caught, kept as tests**, all of which a fingerprint could
not have:

- `tails_of` searched once at the full length, which finds **no tail at all for
  any word** -- a reversed suffix of length *L* sorts *before* the longer reversal
  that extends it, so the suffixes of a word are not one contiguous run. It
  returned nothing for all 696 compounds. Now one search per length.
- `heads_of` collected the `PREFIX` run and the `SHORTEST_HEAD` run and returned
  their overlap twice, which would hand a compound the same head twice and double
  its forms.
- `WORD_ORDER` slicing at four **bytes** panicked on `ala‑`, whose U+2011
  NON-BREAKING HYPHEN is three bytes. Every cut in the file now goes through
  `prefix_chars`, which counts characters.

And a property of the sanalista that two drafts of these tests got wrong: **a
word can be listed more than once as homonym rows** -- `iso` is rows 18450 and
18451 -- so counting returned *words* to detect a duplicate calls correct
behaviour wrong. The duplicate check is on rows, inside the module, where the two
are distinguishable. Five claims in this file have been retracted for reading the
code instead of measuring; this one was retracted by a test.

**No fingerprint change and no re-baseline.** 4 162 084 forms, byte-identical to
`nouns_base_v25`, as a pure performance change must be. Gates unchanged: reverse
lookup 99.88% (625 433/626 201), binding 21/21, compound 15 116/15 310, 656
workspace tests, clippy 0 errors and 0 warnings from the new file.

Touches neither noun tree -- `build.rs`, `expand/mod.rs`, `expand/build.rs`,
`expand/compound.rs`, the binding and one tool -- so no paired commit is needed.

### The gate, threaded (`0c73c14`)

The reverse-lookup gate was 216 s of single-threaded lookup. Same idiom as
`expand::build`: `available_parallelism()`, `BATCH = 1 << 14`, chunks of
`batch.len() / (threads * 8)` with a floor of 64, `std::thread::scope`, results
reassembled **in chunk order** so the TSV keeps input order, and `Tally::room()`
so the global 200-miss cap is honoured across batches in input order rather than
by whichever batch finishes first. `FormBuilder` is `Sync` -- every field is a
`Vec`, a `WordSet` or a `HashMap` of plain data -- so no locking.

**216 s -> 67 s, and the output is byte-identical between one thread and twelve**
over all 626 201 rows: same rows, same order, same miss list. That is the check
that matters, because a threaded tool that reorders its output would still print
the same totals. Tool-only, so no paired commit.

3. **No split at all** (`mustaraamattu`, `suorakulmio`), where the sanalista names
   nothing but the marker. There is no data-driven replacement, and that part of
   this item still stands.
4. **A bracketed class dropped by policy** (`kuivamuona`) and two
   sanalista/reference class disagreements (`vapaamatkustaja`, `raakanahka`).

#### The separate defect underneath them

When a marker yields no split, `inflect_in` falls through to the marker's own
class model, and a model word like `hienonsokeri` aligned against a whole
compound lands the gradation inside it. The output is not merely short, it is
non-words:

    pitkäthousut     ->  iksi, illa, ille, illä, ilta, iltä   (the head, stripped)
    särkynytsydän   ->  särkapareiksi, särkapareilla
    mustaraamattu   ->  musseennoutajaan
    nuori-isäntä    ->  nuori-eenpäriin, nuori-idensärjöjen

`cc0afee` removed this for class 50 by falling back to `head_class`, which reads
the head's class off the same sanalista row. Class 51 has no equivalent and these
rows declare no head class, so there is nothing to fall back to. **The honest
statement is that a compound marker with no split has no data-driven answer**, and
the junk is the fallback doing the only thing it can.

Counted over the whole sanalista (104 263 lemmas): **652** carry class 50 and
**643** of those split; **93** carry class 51 and **91** split. Ten distinct
lemmas have no split at all.

#### Two near-misses that are not findings

`kevytsinko` is 8/9 and `täysihoito` is 8/9, each missing exactly the ablative
plural (`-oilta`) although the tail produces it alone (`singoilta`, `hoidoilta`).
Of the 125 class-50/51 compounds whose tail is a class-1 noun and which have
reference forms, only 3 fall short, and two of those are these. One form on two
words out of 125 is not a systematic gap; do not go looking for the mechanism
without more instances.

### 3. What an `X,Y` list means -- answered, with three residues

This was open item 1 before, and it is now settled by `data/kotus-tyypit.txt`
rather than by guesswork:

> Jos sana taipuu kahdella eri tavalla, sillä on kaksi taivutusnumeroa.
>
> (*If a word inflects in two different ways, it has two declension numbers.*)

So two numbers on an ordinary word means two declensions and **both run** --
which is what `resolve_inflections` does, and what `ori` (`48,5`) requires.
Measured over the 96 coincident-grid fingerprint lemmas that have reference
forms:

    93   the other class adds nothing the reference confirms
     3   neither class alone is enough, the union is needed
         (kaihi 5,7 / karhi 5,7 / palvi 7,5 -- class 5 covers 89%, class 7 adds
          kaiheja, kaiheihin, kaihit)

So "keep both" is right 96 times out of 96 where there is evidence, and the
`40,39` / `21,1` / `9,12` pairings were never a defect. `koala` is `9,12,(10)`,
which also touches item 4 below.

The residues are item 1 (`vihanta`) and item 2 above.

### 4. ~~`parse_single` drops parenthesised classes~~ -- partly answered in `0f12bbb`

This item asked not to guess at what a bracket means. **The sanalista's own
documentation answers it**, at `data/kotus-tyypit.txt:38-50`, and the answer is
that a parenthesis is three different things:

| attribute | value | written as | example |
|---|---|---|---|
| `taivutus` | `harvinainen` (rare), `mahdollinen` (possible) | `(class)` beside a plain one | `koiras 41, (39)` |
| `taivutus` | `yksikössä`, `monikossa` (in the singular/plural) | `(mon.7)` | `kolme 8, (mon.7)` |
| `astevaihtelu` | `valinnainen` (gradation may be applied **or not**) | `8*(C)` | `bourette 8*(C)` |

Plus a fourth shape the documentation does not name: a field that is *only* a
bracketed class is how the sanalista writes a word it does not split.

`0f12bbb` fixed that last one, because it is the only case with no alternative
reading. **43 sanalista rows** had no class at all and `inflect adagio` raised
`no class to inflect with`; `adagio` is `(1)`, `a cappella` is `(9)`, `chic` is
`(5)`, and there is no unbracketed item to prefer, so the bracket is the field.
`adagio` now gives 162 forms including `allegroon`, `a cappellaan`, `chiceihin`.
**No fingerprint change** -- these are all foreign adverbs that the 29 316-lemma
fingerprint, drawn from the kotus noun database, does not contain.

The other three are now *measured* rather than open, and the measurements say
leave them alone:

- **`(class)` beside a plain one stays dropped.** `koiras` is `41, (39)` and
  class 39's model word is a different word, so running it yields `koirakseen`
  where the reference confirms `koiraaseen`. Over the 18 fingerprint lemmas in
  this position, running the bracketed declension gains confirmed forms for
  **three** -- `humala` 13 (`humaloiden`, `humaloihin`, `humaloin`, the `humaloinen`
  paradigm of class 11), `smoothie` 1 (`smoothieita`), `sioux` 1 (`sioux'n`,
  `sioux't`) -- and **none** for the other fifteen, while `salama` gains
  `salamia` and `koala` gains `koalia`, which are not words. 2 310 forms added
  for three lemmas' worth of confirmed ones.
  **The three exceptions are real and one day deserve the class.** A fix would
  have to tell "rare but real" from "near neighbour with the wrong model word",
  and the sanalista gives nothing to tell them apart by. Do not paper over this
  with a word list.
- **`8*(C)` stays as plain `8`.** `valinnainen` says the gradation may be applied
  or not, so both readings exist -- but plain `8` is 100% of the reference in all
  five measurable cases, where the gradation covers 11/25 for `ringette` and
  2/27 for `häive`. Emitting the gradation too would add 3 171 forms and gain
  nothing confirmed.
- **`mon.` is `monikossa`, not a class, and is not honoured.** `kolme` is
  `8, (mon.7)`; the 12 plural forms the reference confirms (`kolmia`, `kolmien`,
  `kolmiin`, ...) need class 7 **with a gradation the sanalista does not give**.
  Reading the marker means inventing the letter, so `kolme` stays class 8 and
  misses those 12. The sanalista's row for this word is incomplete.

Note the earlier revision of this file said `mon.` meant "monikerta". It means
`monikossa`. Both are abbreviations of a `taivutus` attribute and neither is a
class.

### 6. The `class_1.rs` swap -- done, in `6e5a1f8`

Class 1 ships the ported engine and the hand-written file is deleted. Its
`#[path]` points into the refactored tree, so one source file serves both
trees and there is no second copy to drift.

Worth knowing before the next class, because it was not the one-file change it
looked like: **`utility` had to land first.** `class_1.rs` is written against
the `Decl` machinery, which lives in `utility`, so the swap does not compile
until the refactored `utility.rs` is the live tree's -- which moves the
substrate under all 149 classes at once. The refactored `Builder` turned out to
be a strict superset of the live one (every method the live tree had, plus
`decline`, `base_on`, `on_stem`, `on_ending`, `as_rare`, `pick`), so no call
site needed changing. The fingerprint is byte-identical, which is what the port
had to show.

**What it cost:** the equivalence gate no longer says anything about class 1.
It compares `nominals::class_1` against `nominals_refactored::class_1`, which
are now the same file, so class 1's 6 fixture lines -- 12 of the gate's 1 434
cases -- compare the file with itself. Accepted deliberately. Every remaining
class is still compared for real.

Also lost and recovered: the refactored `utility.rs` had **no test module at
all**, so copying it over the live one silently dropped
`a_rare_possessive_says_whose_it_is` and the suite went 581 -> 580. That test
is the regression gate for `cell_rare` sending its possessives through
`push_rare`, which tagged `ehtooheni` as a bare illative across 62 748 forms
over 11 363 lemmas. It is ported across now and runs in both trees (582).
**Check the test count after any future port of a file that had tests** -- the
count is the only thing that notices.

### 7. The `Columns` enum

`Builder` still carries `possessives: bool` with five guards and a read at
`utility.rs`. The standing question is whether form builders should only ever
form one kind of form -- replacing the flag with `Columns::{Bare, Possessed,
Both}` so the two cannot be confused at a call site.

**Recommended against, for now, and less urgently than it was.** Nothing in the
tree constructs `Possessed` alone, so the enum would add a state no code
exercises. The bugs it would have prevented are now fixed and gated directly,
which is worth more than the type change. Revisit only if the flag reads badly
again. The same applies to `build_i_loans(lemma, possessives, front, ipl)`.

  as a real regression, not a baseline to refresh.

  fingerprint is a digest of form counts, so a row with no class and therefore
  no output is invisible to it -- `inflect adagio` raised

  fix that repaired them was byte-identical in the baseline. Re-baselining would

  directly, not through a count.
- **The sanalista's own documentation answers format questions; read it before
  measuring.** `data/kotus-tyypit.txt` settled the 50/51 semantics, the meaning
  of an `X,Y` list, *and* what a bracket in `taivutustiedot` means -- it cost one

  guess at the semantics". Lines 38-50 list the `taivutus` and `astevaihtelu`
  attribute values.
- **The fingerprint is blind to the reverse direction, for the same structural
  reason it is blind to compounds.** It digests how many forms each lemma makes.
  A form that goes into the index and comes back attached to the wrong base
  produces exactly as many forms as before, so every defect fixed in items 12 and
  13 of this round would have passed a green fingerprint forever. The three
  original gates -- the digest, the tree equivalence, and the possessive column --
  could not have found the `merge` defect even one of them was built to. Use
  `tools/test_reverse_lookup_recall.py`.
- **A green fingerprint means one of two things and the file should say which.**
  Either nothing changed, or the gate was never looking at the thing that
  changed. Three commits in a row here have been the second kind
  (`cc0afee`, `ba4b738`, and the reverse-lookup round), and the trap is that the
  second kind feels exactly like the first once you have seen it twice.
- **The fingerprint is blind to compound defects, for a structural reason.** It
  draws its lemmas from the kotus noun database, and **only 22 of the 695
  class-50/51 compound-marker lemmas in the sanalista appear there at all** --
  zero of the 16 that `ba4b738` fixed. Both compound fixes so far (`cc0afee`,
  `ba4b738`) left the digest byte-identical. So a green fingerprint says nothing
  about this part of the engine, and "no re-baseline needed" here means "the gate
  was never looking", not "nothing changed". Use `tools/compound_recall.py`.
- **A `FIX_ME.md` claim of "the data does not say" is a claim, not a finding.**
  This file asserted for several commits that six compounds had no principled
  answer available. One of them (`puoliyö`) turned out to be decided by a
  three-letter minimum in `find_parts` -- a code fact, not a data gap. When
  something is missing, go and look at whether the thing that is missing is
  *missing* or merely *not being looked for*; a boundary condition reads exactly
  like absent data from the outside.
- **Measure the union of classes, not one class at a time.** `isoviha` class 50
  alone is 1/23 against the reference, and `inflect('isoviha')` covers 23/23
  because the word is `51, 50` and class 51 carries it. A per-class measurement
  reports the weaker class as broken and inflates the defect count by 3x.
- **Two-letter lemmas are mostly not nouns.** Of the 48 the sanalista spells with
  two letters, 23 carry a declension and 25 do not -- ten pronouns, fourteen
  interjections, an adverb, a conjunction. Any code that matches sanalista lemmas
  by *shape* rather than by *class* will pick up `sa`, `ma`, `te`, `ai`, `ha`.
  This is the same lesson as `parse_inflections` and the brackets, one column over.
- **A shared-prefix prefilter for the reverse lookup is unsound. Measured, do not
  retry.** The idea is to skip lemmas that cannot share a prefix with the word,
  which would take 634 146 candidate pairs down to a few thousand. The first
  attempt -- `form.startswith(&lemma[..k])` for `k >= 1` -- is nearly vacuous,
  since `k == 1` only compares the first letter, so it has to be measured rather
  than reasoned about. Measured over all pairs: the minimum shared-prefix ratio
  is **0.000**; **193** pairs share one letter or none; and the ones that share
  none include `yö` -> `öiden`, an initial-`y` umlaut. Adding a `y` -> `o`-umlaut

  **not** guaranteed even for literal-stem classes. For reference, a sound
  LCP>=1 prefilter narrows 47 788 distinct lemmas to roughly 6 000-15 000 and
  LCP>=2 to 200-2 700 -- the sound floor is also the useless one, and the useless
  one is the one that would be correct. Not implemented.

### 9. `class_99` runs the `-i` loan machinery over words that are not `-i` loans

This is what item 15's `99` half runs into, and the reason class-99 forms are
excluded from the index rather than included.

`class_99.rs` offers the bare lemma, and then calls `build_i_loans` on every
class-99 word outside its own list of the genuinely indeclinable ones
(`no_inflect`, 130 words). `i_stem` appends `i` unconditionally, so the `-i`
paradigm lands on any word at all:

```
ainut  ->  ainut, ainuteiden, ainuteihin, ainuteiksi, ainuteilla, ...   (29)
karku  ->  karku, karkueidemme, karkueiden, karkueihimme, ...           (183)
ha     ->  ha, haeiden, haeihin, haeiksi, haeilla, ...                 (29)
```

None of those are Finnish words. `ainuteiden` is the `-iden`/`-itten`/`-ita`
alternant set, which is why `class_99` passes `IPlural::All` -- the only class
besides 5 and 6 to do so. Classes 5, 6 and 22 can: every member of those *is* an
`-i` loan by definition of the class.

**What the reference says, measured both ways.** Of the 5 724 class-99-only
sanalista lemmas, **28 have any reference form at all**, and of the 291 forms
those carry the engine confirms 92. Ten lemmas are confirmed as i-loans (`miss`,
`seesam`, `aprilli`, `ani`, `avo`, `liki`, `melko`, `sori`, `vai`, and `presto`
in part), which is what `build_i_loans` is right about. The other 18 want things
it is not: `kera` -> `keralla`, `kukku` -> `kukkua`, `kuulema` -> `kuulemaa`,
`ha` -> 22 clitic forms like `ha:han`, and `ainut` -> `ainutta`.

**So the noun grid under `build_i_loans` was measured, not assumed.** Patching out
the `Builder::new` call -- which the module's own doc says should not be there
("they carry no paradigm of their own and are offered exactly as the lemma") --
changed `miss` from 183 forms to 178 and confirmed forms from 92 to **92**. The
`Builder::new` output is entirely subsumed by `build_i_loans`, so removing it is a
simplification and not a fix. It was reverted rather than committed: a refactor
with no measured benefit is not worth a baseline's worth of diff.

**A narrow measurement nearly produced a wrong retraction here.** Asked whether
the reference confirms any `-iden`/`-itten`/`-ita` for class-99 words, using the
filters the recall gate uses (`ok='both'`, sanalista-class-99-only), the answer is
**zero** -- which reads as a clean case for `IPlural::None`. Widening the query to
every reference lemma whose own class is `99`, with no `ok` filter, finds **83**
such rows over 22 lemmas (`pai`, `voi`, `joka`, `vai`, `tai`, `ainut`,
`sivusta`, `toiste`). `IPlural::All` is correct and was left alone. The narrow
answer was an artefact of the filters, not a finding.

**What is left is mostly not an engine defect at all, and saying so changes what
the gate's residual means.** Taking the 199 class-99 misses where the engine
generates no such form and sorting them by what the reference actually wants:

    148  ordinary nouns the sanalista marks uninflected
          kuulema 24, käsikynkkä 24, pirulauta 24, käsikoukku 23, perin 25,
          näin 14, kyse 5, kukku 5, tenä 4
     23  clitic forms of `ha` (`ha:han`, `ha:lla`, ...) -- not nominal declension
     15  `presto`, where the reference wants the noun paradigm and we give the
          `-i` loan one
     13  postpositions and partials: `luo`, `kera`, `karku`, `ainut`, `ja`,
          `soopa`, `tai`, `tarvis`

**148 of 199 -- 74% -- are a sanalista gap, not a bug.** `pirulauta` is a
getting-jointed saw and `kuulema` is a hearing; the reference tabulates both as
24-form nouns, and the sanalista gives both the number that means "I have no
paradigm for this". There is nothing to inflect them from, so no amount of work
here recovers them. `perin` and `näin` are worse: the reference lists two stems
for each (`perin-` and `perim-`/`perint-`, `näin-` and `näki-`), every form twice,
which is a homonym pair and not one paradigm.

**One hypothesis of mine was wrong and is retracted here rather than left in the
file.** `kotus-tyypit.txt:55` says "uninflected **or only partly inflected**",
and the single-form entries do look like a productive case -- `ainut` ->
`ainutta`, `ja` -> `jana`, `tai` -> `taita`. Reading the reference's own `case`
column kills it: `ainutta`, `soopaa`, `tarvista` and `taita` are labelled
**partitive**, and only `jana` and `sorina` are labelled **essive**. Across all
320 class-99-only reference rows the label spread is flat -- 24 `(case, number)`
cells, the largest holding 19 -- so no single case is what this class owes. That
would have been a fourth "the data does not say" claim of my own making, and it
was one filter-deep from being recorded as a finding.

Until the sanalista gains those paradigms, the honest position is the one item 15's
predicate takes: class-99 forms stay **out** of the index. Measured over the 42
marker-only lemmas that have reference forms at all, the engine generates 2 721
forms for them and the reference confirms 223 -- **2 498 are guesses**. Being
excluded is the safer error; a base form that is a guess is worse than no answer.

## Breaking API changes since the last release

- **`load_sanalista` is gone.** With `Lemma` borrowing, no function can return
  parsed rows alongside the text they point into. Use `sanalista()` for the
  embedded lexicon, or `read_sanalista(path)` + `parse_sanalista(&text)` for a
  custom one.
- **`Lemma` gained a lifetime** (`Lemma<'a>`) and its three text fields are now
  `&str` rather than `String`.
- **The generated class modules take a second argument.** `class_49D`,
  `class_49E`, `class_49F`, `class_49G`, `class_49H`, `class_49J`, `class_50`
  and `class_51` changed from `inflect(lemma)` to `inflect(lemma, possessives)`.
- **`Engine.inflect`'s class argument is `klass`, not `class`.** The old name
  was a reserved word and so could never be passed as a keyword argument
  (`SyntaxError`, not `TypeError`). Positional callers are unaffected.
- **New: `expand::inflect_all`.** `inflect_in` is one class; a caller with a
  resolved class list wants this one.
- **New: `torille_tools::resolve_inflections`.** A caller wanting the sanalista's
  own resolution rather than torille's should use this, not re-derive it.
- **New: `tools/compound_recall.py`.** Reference recall over every class-50/51
  compound. The fingerprint covers only 22 of the 695, so this is the only gate
  that can see this part of the engine.

## Traps worth writing down

- **`cargo build` does not update the Python binding.** Rebuild with
  `(cd bindings && env -u CONDA_PREFIX maturin develop --release)`. And check
  the installed `.so` is actually newer than your edit -- `maturin develop`
  prints a wall of `cargo` warnings and the success line is easy to miss, so a
  stale extension module reads exactly like a fix that did not work.
  This is not hypothetical: item 14's first fingerprint run reported the digest
  **unchanged** with **zero** lines differing, which looked like a fourth blind
  spot in the fingerprint. It was not -- `cargo build -p torille` had rebuilt the
  library and `fingerprint_nouns.py` goes through the binding. After
  `maturin develop` the same command moved 23 digests. **A gate reporting "no
  change" after a fix is evidence about the gate until proven otherwise**, so
  rebuild everything the gate actually loads before believing it.
- **`cargo test` at the repo root only tests `torille`.** The workspace root is
  also a package, so the `bindings` and `tools` tests are skipped silently. Use
  `cargo test --release --workspace`.
- **`bindings`' tests were not running at all until `c43889a`.** `crate-type`
  was `cdylib` only and pyo3's `extension-module` was a default feature, so the
  test binary had no rlib to link and then died on
  `undefined symbol: PyExc_ValueError`. `extension-module` is now opt-in and
  maturin asks for it. If the binding tests stop running, check both.
- **`parse_pos` takes the Finnish POS strings**, not English ones.
  `inflect talo 1 noun` silently yields `PartOfSpeech::Unknown` and therefore no
  possessives; `inflect talo 1 substantiivi` is what you want. The same applies
  to the Python `pos=` argument.
- **`rustfmt` follows `mod` declarations** -- never pass `mod.rs`, or it
  reorders the `pub mod` list. Never rustfmt the live tree's hand-written class
  modules either. Never rustfmt `nominals-refactored/mod.rs`; same reason, and
  it has bitten before.
- **`rustfmt src/expand/mod.rs` reformats the whole noun tree, and it is not
  reversible by `git checkout` on the trees alone.** It rewrote 100+ files,
  `class_50.rs` by 14 142 lines. Worse, it also reflowed ~200 lines of
  *pre-existing* code inside `mod.rs` itself, so reverting only the trees still
  left a 393-line diff there. The recovery is: save the two blocks you added,
  `git checkout -- src/expand/mod.rs src/expand/nominals
  src/expand/nominals-refactored src/expand/verbs src/expand/build.rs
  src/expand/compound.rs`, and re-apply by hand. Then confirm with
  `git diff --stat` that only your own files are listed.
- **`rustfmt` needs `--edition 2024` on the noun trees.** The crate is edition
  2024 and `utility.rs` uses let-chains, so `rustfmt --edition 2021` fails with
  "let chains are only allowed in Rust 2024 or later". For the binding crate use
  `cargo fmt --manifest-path bindings/Cargo.toml` -- `-p torille_py` from the
  root fails, saying it is not a workspace member.
- **Shipping a ported class shrinks the equivalence gate, silently.** Once a
  class's live module is `#[path]`-pointed at its refactored twin, the gate
  compares the file with itself and still reports its full 1 434 cases, because
  it counts fixture lines rather than distinct comparisons. Class 1 costs 12
  cases today. The `cases > 1_400` floor will not catch this; it has to be
  reasoned about, or the gate made to skip classes that are shared.
- **A ported file may have lost its tests.** The refactored `utility.rs` had no
  `mod tests` at all, so overwriting the live one dropped
  `a_rare_possessive_says_whose_it_is` and nothing failed except the count
  going 581 -> 580. Compare `cargo test --release --lib | grep '^test result'`
  before and after any file swap.
- **Filter the reference and the output the same way, or the coverage figure is
  meaningless.** Only 25 of `hapanjuuri`'s 159 confirmed forms are
  non-possessive, so measuring non-possessive output against an unfiltered
  reference pins *every* compound's coverage at 16% and makes the measurement
  look like a finding. Always
  `WHERE ok='both' AND possessive=''` on the reference side and
  `if not f.possessive` on the output side.
- **The reference's `ok` column has only two values**, `'both'` and `'omorfi'`.
  Only `ok='both'` is double-confirmed; a form absent from it is *unconfirmed*,
  not wrong. So **recall against `ok='both'` is the only sound measure --
  precision cannot be measured this way at all.** Checking recall only is what
  nearly produced the wrong "keep every listed class" conclusion; a precision
  spot-check on printed output shapes caught it.
- **A `break`/loop variable that leaks out of a `for` is a measurement bug, not
  a finding.** The first version of the compound measurement reported `nuoripari`
  at 0% coverage when it is 96%, because the second loop reused the first's
  `ref`. When a number surprises you, re-derive it in three lines before
  believing it.
- **`Gradation` counts `A..Z` as `1..=26`,** so `Gradation('C' as u8)` is 67,
  not 3. Go through `Gradation::from_letter`. Getting this wrong makes a test
  compare class 32 against class 32*something-else and fail with a plausible
  message.
- `inflect` is **not** `target/release/torille` -- that is a 13-byte
  `fn main() {}` stub. The CLIs need `-p torille-tools`.
- `build.rs` re-parses `data/nykysuomensanalista.txt` with its own copy of the
  inflection grammar. `the_embedded_lexicon_is_the_parsed_lexicon` compares all
  104 743 rows field-for-field to keep the two honest -- if you touch the
  grammar in `kotus.rs`, that test is what will tell you the build script
  disagrees.
- Homonyms are read with `any`, not `first`. `aika` is a `substantiivi` in
  class 9*D *and* an `adverb` in class 99, so it keeps its possessives. Do not
  "simplify" a POS check to take the first row: that is the defect `9ad8772`
  fixed, and `PartOfSpeech::inflecting_reading` is now the one place the rule
  lives.
